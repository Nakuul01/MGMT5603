UPI × DuitNow QR

How a partnership between India and Malaysia turns a familiar QR payment into a global-fintech strategy.

✦

The key idea:
UPI can enter a foreign market by connecting with trusted local payment infrastructure, not by replacing it.

The news

What happened?

NPCI International Payments Ltd (NIPL), the international arm of India’s National Payments Corporation of India, partnered with Payments Network Malaysia (PayNet) to enable cross-border QR merchant payments between India’s UPI and Malaysia’s DuitNow QR network.

IN

India: UPI

UPI is India’s real-time payment system for bank-to-bank transfers and merchant QR payments through mobile apps.

↔

Interoperability

The two systems are designed to connect so users can pay across borders using familiar domestic payment tools.

MY

Malaysia: DuitNow QR

DuitNow QR is Malaysia’s established QR payment network, used by merchants and local payment applications.

The customer journey

How the payment can work

Example: an Indian traveller in Malaysia

The initial phase enables Indian visitors to pay Malaysian DuitNow QR merchants with UPI-enabled apps.

1

Travel

An Indian visitor arrives in Malaysia.

2

Open UPI app

They use an existing UPI-enabled payment app.

3

Scan

They scan the Malaysian merchant’s DuitNow QR code.

4

Connect

UPI and DuitNow communicate through the cross-border payment link.

5

Pay

The Malaysian merchant receives payment through its familiar local network.

Why this matters: the traveller keeps a familiar Indian app, while the merchant keeps a familiar Malaysian QR system.

Global strategy analysis

Why use a partnership?

Benefits of partnership-based entry

  • Uses an existing local merchant-payment network.
  • Can reduce the cost and time of foreign-market entry.
  • Supports local acceptance and trust in a regulated sector.
  • Grows network effects across users, banks, merchants, and countries.
  • Improves payment convenience for travellers and cross-border commerce.
!

Challenges to manage

  • Financial regulation and compliance in both countries.
  • Cybersecurity, fraud prevention, and data privacy.
  • Foreign-exchange conversion and transaction costs.
  • Merchant and traveller awareness and adoption.
  • Competition from cards, wallets, and other payment systems.

Strategic lesson

Technology alone does not make global expansion successful.

UPI’s Malaysia strategy shows that global fintech expansion also depends on interoperability, trusted local partners, regulatory fit, and an easy customer experience.
